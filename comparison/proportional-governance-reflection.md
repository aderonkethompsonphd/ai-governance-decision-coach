# Proportional governance reflection

## Why these cases require different decisions

The recruitment system and meeting-summary tool both use AI, but the presence of AI alone does not determine the governance response.

The recruitment system analyses and ranks people and may automatically exclude them from employment opportunities. It operates at scale, relies on personal and historical data and creates potential discrimination, contestability and transparency problems. Human involvement is weak because recruiters normally review only highly ranked applicants and revisit rejected applications mainly after complaints. The appropriate decision is to pause for evidence and redesign before considering a restricted pilot.

The meeting-summary tool is assistive. It does not decide, rank, recommend or trigger actions, and its outputs are intended to receive employee review. Its effects are more reversible and its purpose can be met through a manual fallback. Approval with conditions is therefore proportionate, provided the organisation verifies data handling, vendor commitments, accuracy, meaningful review, correction and exit controls.

## Comparative reasoning

| Dimension | Applicant screening | Meeting summarisation |
|---|---|---|
| Effect on people | May determine access to employment | Supports internal documentation |
| Inherent risk | High | Moderate |
| Human oversight | Weak and complaint dependent | Promising but not yet evidenced |
| Data | Personal, historical and potentially proxy-rich | Intended to exclude sensitive categories, but accidental entry remains plausible |
| Contestability | Rejected applicants may not understand or challenge outcomes | Employees can correct drafts if the process is effective |
| Reversibility | Harm may already affect opportunities | Tool can be stopped and manual summarisation restored |
| Recommended decision | Pause and redesign before a restricted pilot | Approve with conditions |

## Transferable lessons

1. Assess the consequences and context of the use, not the technology label alone.
2. Human presence does not automatically constitute meaningful oversight.
3. Vendor statements and planned safeguards are not operating evidence.
4. Residual risk cannot be concluded until controls are implemented and tested.
5. Lower-impact AI still requires clear scope, ownership, monitoring, change management and exit arrangements.
